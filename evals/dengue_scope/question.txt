SYNTHETIC TEST QUESTION (evaluation suite): the city, numbers and files below are synthetic, generated for this test; they do not describe a real place. In 2025 Synthetic City (population 1.2 million) had its first DENV-2 outbreak in many years. Input files: data/evals/dengue_scope/city.csv, data/evals/dengue_scope/weekly_reported_cases_2025.csv, data/evals/dengue_scope/generation_interval.csv (the distribution of the time between successive human infections, in weeks). No serosurvey is available, and the fraction of infections that are reported is not known (published ratios of reported cases to infections for dengue vary widely, roughly 1 in 3 to 1 in 30). The city must decide whether to fund a pre-season DENV-2 control campaign for 2026. Using a simple SIR-type model of human infection (vector dynamics absorbed into the transmission rate), estimate (1) the effective reproduction number of DENV-2 at the start of the 2026 season, assuming the same transmissibility as in 2025 and no births, deaths, waning immunity or migration, and (2) the proportion of the city's population infected with DENV-2 in 2025. Report each with a 95% uncertainty interval, say whether a large DENV-2 outbreak is expected in 2026, and state what the data do and do not determine. Deliverables: the uncertainty analysis must report outputs named reff_2026 and attack_rate_2025 (attack_rate_2025 as a proportion of the whole population).
