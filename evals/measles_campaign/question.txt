SYNTHETIC TEST QUESTION (evaluation suite): every district, number and file below is synthetic, generated for this test; none describes a real place. A measles programme has US$850,000 for a catch-up vaccination campaign in early 2026 across 12 districts. For each district choose one option: none; A_1to4 (all children aged 1-4 years); B_1to9 (all children aged 1-9 years). A campaign reaches 85% of targeted children and seroconverts 93% of the susceptible children it reaches. Cost: US$1.20 per targeted child plus US$22,000 per district campaign; each single-year birth cohort is 3.0% of a district's population. Routine MCV1 seroconverts about 85% of the children it reaches and MCV2 about 90% of those still susceptible. Assume everyone aged 15 years or older is immune and children under 1 year are susceptible, and that in each district one measles importation in the coming period starts an outbreak whose size follows the final-size relation of a homogeneous-mixing SIR model, with R0 between 12 and 18 (uniform). Input files: data/evals/measles_campaign/districts.csv (2026 population), data/evals/measles_campaign/routine_coverage_admin.csv (administrative MCV1 and MCV2 coverage by birth cohort), data/evals/measles_campaign/serosurvey_2025.csv (a 2025 serosurvey by age band; ages are as in 2025). Choose the options that maximise expected measles cases averted, report the total with a 95% uncertainty interval, and state the decision rule. Deliverables: allocation_optimized.csv in the run directory with columns district, package (none, A_1to4 or B_1to9) and cases_averted; and the uncertainty analysis must report the total as an output named total_cases_averted.
