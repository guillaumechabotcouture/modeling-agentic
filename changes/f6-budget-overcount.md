## Changelog

**Finished clusters count what they billed, and their reservations are released (F6 S2-BG3).** On 2026-11-11 at 18:20, `budget.py status --scope f6` showed $306 used against $191.68 billed, and $67 still reserved for a launch whose run, keeper and overflow clusters (`v2-nigeria-gf13-p6`, ids 2364928, 2364962, 2364947) were all stopped. That blocked further launches. Two causes, both in `_cluster_cost`. First, every cluster counted max(billed, estimate), and the estimate (VM-type upper bounds with margin) is about 1.6 x what Coiled bills, so finished clusters were over-counted. Second, a liveness check that read instance states. A stopped cluster's record can keep a VM in a non-final state (an overflow VM refused for quota stays `queued`), which read as live. That VM was projected to the present, and it held the launch's reservation at max(0, cap - spend) instead of releasing it. The fix: a cluster whose every process is stopped or errored, with its stop time, is finished. It counts its billed USD (`total_cost` x 0.061 + `cloud_total_cost`), and its VM records end with their processes. If a cost is missing, it counts the conservative estimate, and its partial bill is left out of the measured rate. A cluster with a live instance still counts max(billed, estimate), and still refuses a missing cost. A reservation whose clusters are all finished is released. Self-tests were written first and failed before the change. BR19 reproduces the P6 case: run, keeper and overflow stopped, one overflow VM left `queued`; the reservation is released and spend equals billed. BR8 checks that a finished cluster counts its bill, BR8e covers a missing cloud cost, and BR9 that a missing cost refuses only while an instance is live. A third cause showed after that fix, still live: $209.83 reserved while only the 18:21 P6 relaunch (cap $120) ran. Each P6 launch (13:03, 15:35, 18:21) had reserved under the same config name while the one before still ran. The relaunch's clusters, having the same names and being created after the earlier reservations, held those reservations too. A reservation is now bound to its own launch. `launch_budget_ok(..., invocation=, clusters_file=)` records the launcher's invocation token, and `run_cluster.py launch` and `keeper` pass theirs and the config's clusters.json. When that clusters.json records cluster ids for the reservation's invocation, the reservation reconciles against those ids only. Otherwise it reconciles against the clusters of its names created after it and before the next reservation of the same name. Two reservations made in the same second cannot be told apart this way and are both held (safe). BR20 reproduces the sequence: three launches of one name, each reserving while the one before runs, the first two stopped, so only the third is held. BR20b covers concurrent launches told apart by clusters.json ids, BR20c the fallback, and BU4d that `launch` passes its invocation and clusters.json.
