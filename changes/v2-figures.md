## Changelog

**v2 figures: a figure set for the decision, drawn from saved artifacts (design: `docs/design/v2-figures.md`).** The harness wrote three figures per run, and the owner judged all three unfit for a report: one interval as a whole figure, bars of internal package codes, and a tornado chart of parameter codes with offset notation and swapping colours. `toolkit/analysis/figures.py` now renders, from the run's artifacts only, up to eleven figures. They are: a compact headline with its interval; the allocation (each unit at its coordinates, or a by-region dot strip without them, with small multiples per component when the plan has more than three packages (the palette is validated on all pairs for three)); assignment stability (the whole package and each component's yes/no over the uncertainty draws); comparator burden per person; the calibration fit; the efficiency frontier (the plan re-solved at 25–150% of the envelope, with the marginal cost per outcome at the envelope); dollars by component and by region against the envelope and fixed costs; cost per outcome averted by package (with its interval) and by component; sensitivity (display names, tested range with units, low and high end in two fixed colours, a marker on rows that change the plan, sorted by effect); a delivery timeline; and modelled trajectories. A figure whose inputs are missing gets a `not_produced` entry with the reason in `analysis/figures.yaml`; nothing is skipped silently. Every axis uses one formatter (`figure_style.fmt`: `12.3M`, `$1.2M`, `45%`), so no figure shows offset notation. The title states the takeaway, and the caption (at most two lines) lives in figures.yaml. Colours come from the dataviz reference palette, validated in light and dark mode. Each figure is written as a 180-dpi PNG, an SVG and a CSV table view, with the provenance sidecar kept. Display names come from an unsigned `presentation.yaml` beside the spec (or `--labels`), never from the signed spec; without them, a figure shows a readable fallback with a definition line. The new artifact `analysis/figure_data.yaml` (`toolkit/analysis/figure_data.py`) holds the numbers the decision figures need: the frontier, dollars, per-package cost per outcome with its interval, component increments, burden and the schedule. They are computed with the run's own emulator and `Analysis.solve`, with no engine runs. The uncertainty draws are replayed with `uncertainty.Drawer`, and the replay must reproduce the stored interval or the package intervals are withheld. `python -m toolkit.analysis.figures render <run_dir> --out <dir> [--recompute]` re-renders a finished run without touching it. `--recompute` rebuilds figure_data from the stored batch, refuses another engine identity, and requires the rebuilt plan to equal the stored one. `compare <run_dir> ...` puts runs of one project side by side and names the spec differences that bear on the headline. Optional engine hooks for the timeline and the trajectories are in `toolkit/engines/hooks.py`: `schedule()`, and `run_series()` with `outputs["series"]` on the card. They fail closed with `HookUnsupported` / `HookInvalid`, and `contract.py` is unchanged, so no engine's fingerprint moves. The trajectory step `python -m toolkit.analysis.trajectories run <run_dir>` runs a stratified unit sample through `scripts/compute.py`. The malaria engine's implementation of both hooks, with its re-stamp proof, is a separate PR. Tests were written first on synthetic toy runs: figures F1–F15, figure_data FD1–FD13, trajectories TJ1–TJ8, hooks H1–H8, style S1–S8, and spec SP-P1–P4.

**Review round 1, three tiers, labels and boundaries.**
- **Codex round 1.** All 17 findings were fixed, each reproduced by a failing test first (F16–F27, FD14–FD18, H9–H12, TJ9–TJ13). An offline rebuild takes a row only when one input within 4 ulps reproduces its job hash, and checks every row's engine identity and the batch files' hashes. Plans are guarded against model corrections. The fit is never invented. Trajectory grids must match and be current. The hooks contract is versioned (`figures-hooks/1`).
- **Three tiers (owner).** Every figure records `tier: data_model | simulation | decision`, and each tier gets an overview sheet. New tier-1 figures: data coverage and provenance, unit attributes, parameters, design coverage. New tier-2 figures: burn-in, seasonality, seed noise, and checks paired with the trajectories. New tier-3 figure: decision tests. A declared `DECISION_SETS` table maps the deliverable kind to its decision figures, and an undeclared kind fails closed. Tests TT1–TT6, TJ14–TJ16.
- **Labels file (owner).** The spec refuses a `presentation` block (SP-P1). Labels files are checked (`labels_problems`), and their path and sha are recorded in each figure's provenance (PL1–PL3).
- **Boundaries (owner).** Optional GeoJSON boundaries, read with json and numpy and matched directly or through a crosswalk. Unmatched units are listed, context features are drawn grey, region outlines are heavier, and the source is credited (BD1–BD3).

**Review round 2.** Each Codex round-2 finding was reproduced by a failing test first:
- BD4–BD6c: crosswalk cardinality and declared unions, empty geometries, geographic CRS only.
- FD14, FD19: ulp tolerance only on the derived field.
- SP-P5: presentation block refused at sign-off.
- TJ17–TJ18: trajectory freshness by full engine identity and inputs; rate labels.
- F28–F34: every sensitivity range kept, explicit deliverable kind, gated compare, presentation files in checked provenance, half-open weighted window means, comparison output and provenance.
- H13–H14d: partial final monthly step; half-open point events.
