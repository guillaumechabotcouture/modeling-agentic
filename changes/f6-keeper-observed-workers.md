## Changelog

**The keeper keeps the attached Cluster's worker view current, so autoscaling no longer retires busy workers.** On 2026-11-12 the P6 preview's first benchmark batch (42 runs of about an hour) took 3.4 h and resubmitted 17 runs; on the resumed launch the scheduler's own events showed workers retired by adaptive scaling with a `_remote_task` chunk still processing (`remove-worker` with non-empty `processing-tasks`), although `ma-compute-busy-guard` was registered on that scheduler. Read-only probes of the keeper process found the cause: its `coiled.Cluster` object observed 5 workers (the pool when it attached) while 31 were requested and the scheduler had 31; the keeper's own per-tick read of the scheduler showed 41. dask's `AdaptiveCore.recommendations` closes workers that are requested but not observed ("not yet arrived") without calling `workers_to_close`, so `BusyWorkerGuard.valid_workers_downscaling` was never consulted for them, and any shrink of the target retired workers in the middle of their chunks. `cloud/worker_side.py` `sync_observed` adds, on every keeper tick, the scheduler's workers the Cluster's view lacks, on the Cluster's event loop. It never replaces or removes (Codex review round 1, both reproduced on distributed 2026.8.0: a replacement could drop a worker that joined after the snapshot, which Adaptive could then retire the same way, and could make the Cluster's own status stream `del` a missing key and stop). The keeper's `cl.adapt` now uses `guarded_adaptive()` (Codex round 2: the 60 s tick left a window, as Adaptive decides every 15 s, in which a worker that joined after the tick could be retired as not yet arrived): before each decision it adds every worker the scheduler has to the view; after it, a worker to retire that the scheduler has goes only if `workers_to_close` (BusyWorkerGuard) offers it, one the scheduler does not have goes, and an unreadable scheduler retires nothing. On a local cluster with the view emptied, plain Adaptive recommended retiring the busy worker and the idle one; the guarded one only the idle one. Self-tests RC52p-r (written first; they failed on the missing function). Why the Cluster's own worker-status stream stopped adding workers (its watch task was alive and its comm open) is not established; the fix does not depend on it. Self-test RC52k (`cloud/run_cluster.py`), written first, failed before the change; after the Codex round it also checks that a worker the snapshot lacks is kept, and RC52l (loop callback, add-only, removals still possible) was written with that change. Live check on P6, with the owner's approval: the same table was written into the running keeper once at 16:38 UTC; in the ten minutes before, 3 of 8 retired workers were busy, and the removals after it were all idle. No other cloud run.
